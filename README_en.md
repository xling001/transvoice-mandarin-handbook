## Core Theoretical Foundations

1. **Anatomical Physiology & Biomechanics**:
   - Theodore Dimon's *Anatomy of the Voice: An Illustrated Guide for Singers, Vocal Coaches, and Speech Therapists* and the Alexander Technique of postural mechanics.
   - Antagonistic breath support, non-forced airflow, Cricothyroid (CT) vs. Thyroarytenoid (TA) muscular coordination, and false vocal fold (FVF) retraction.
2. **Modern TransVoice Acoustic Paradigm**:
   - SumianVoice / TransVoice-Wiki framework (Voice Resource Project) based on psychoacoustics and de-pathologized microbehaviour shaping.
   - Dual-pillar model: **Vocal Weight** (glottal configuration) + **Vocal Size / Resonance** (vocal tract length & volume), evaluated via the 4-Quadrant Fullness Grid.
3. **Pioneering Chinese Community Heritage**:
   - **Aoi's MtF Voice Training Manual** ([https://mtf-voice-training.vercel.app/](https://mtf-voice-training.vercel.app/)): Source-filter theory, 3-step laryngeal elevation mechanics, "water-balloon tongue" model, and baseline Mandarin vowel formant gender contrasts.
   - **TransVoice-Wiki Chinese Translation Project** ([https://transvoice-wiki.pages.dev/translate/zh_Hans/](https://transvoice-wiki.pages.dev/translate/zh_Hans/)): Popularization of the acoustic dual-pillar model and fullness quadrants in Greater China.
4. **Mandarin Experimental Phonetics**:
   - Zhao's 5-Point Pitch Contours (五度标记法), tone sandhi, voice onset time (VOT) contrast, and spectral centroid analysis of Mandarin's three independent sibilant series ([s], [ʂ], [ɕ]); adult vowel space references (Huang et al., 2024).
5. **Clinical Speech-Language Pathology & GAVT**:
   - Transitioning from vocal gymnastics to modern **Gender-Affirming Voice and Communication** clinical assessment, vocal hygiene red flags, and daily life generalization.

## Chapter Overview

### Introduction: Cognitive Pivot — Beyond "Rigid Pitch Elevation"
Exposes the common pitfall where learners force fundamental frequency ($F_0$) above 220 Hz via throat squeezing, resulting in strain, dysphonia, and an unnatural "falsetto" timbre. Introduces the three cognitive transitions:
1. *From "Pitch Primacy" to "Acoustic Dual Pillars (Weight & Size)"*;
2. *From "Laryngeal Muscles" to "Holistic Somatic Release"*;
3. *From "Passable Exam" to "Gender-Affirming Communication"*.

### Chapter 1: Anatomy & Physiology of the Vocal Generator
- **1.1 Power System**: Diaphragmatic recoil, intercostal antagonistic support, and steady non-forced aerodynamic flow.
- **1.2 Vibration Source**: Thyroid, cricoid, and arytenoid cartilage framework; CT (pitch-stretching / thinning) vs. TA (pitch-lowering / thickening) coordination.
- **1.3 Tension Structures**: False Vocal Folds (ventricular folds) and laryngeal ventricles; mechanics of ventricular constriction ("pressed voice") and silent-laughter retraction.
- **1.4 Suspension & Resonance**: Hyoid bone sling system and the concept of "free laryngeal floating" versus swallowing constriction.

### Chapter 2: Modern TransVoice Acoustic Model (Weight & Space)
- **2.1 Two Perceptual Pillars**: Vocal Weight (perceived thinness/flow vs. thickness/heaviness) and Vocal Size (perceived tract brightness/compactness vs. darkness/spaciousness).
- **2.2 Vocal Weight**: Glottal contact quotient, $H_1-H_2$ spectral tilt, moving away from pressed closure toward flow phonation.
- **2.3 Vocal Size / Resonance**: Formant spacing $\Delta F$, tongue back positioning, pharyngeal narrowing vs. dangerous hypopharyngeal squeeze.
- **2.4 4-Quadrant Fullness Grid**: Dissecting Hollow (Underfull), Overfull (Bright-Heavy), Pressed/Pinched, and Affirmed/Balanced voice coordinates.
- **2.5 Microbehaviours & Mimicry**: Subconscious pitch glide trajectories, soft onsets, and vowel prolongation patterns.

### Chapter 3: Mandarin Chinese Acoustic Challenges & Localization
- **3.1 Intonation vs. Tonal Language**: Unlike stress-timed English, Mandarin relies on lexically contrastive tonal pitch contours (55, 35, 214, 51), creating frequent pitch collisions.
- **3.2 Avoiding the "Vocal Fry & Squeeze Trap"**:
  - **Tone 3 (上声 214)**: Introducing **Tone Floating (软上声高位托底)** — keeping the pitch dip above $150\sim 165\text{ Hz}$ in everyday speech (211 curve) without crashing into heavy creaky voice.
  - **Tone 4 (去声 51)**: Introducing **Soft Landing (去声浅降)** — 53 or 52 glides rather than steep 51 drops that trigger TA heavy clamping.
- **3.3 Sibilants & Aspiration**:
  - Distinguishing Dental `[s]`, Retroflex `[ʂ]`, and Alveolo-palatal `[ɕ]` spectral centroids;
  - **Laminal articulation** to prevent tongue root retraction;
  - VOT (Voice Onset Time) clarity on aspirated stops (`p`, `t`, `k`).
- **3.4 Vowel Space & $F_3$ Lip Decoupling**:
  - Six monophthong vowel formants ($F_1, F_2, F_3$);
  - Distinguishing `/i/` from `/ü/` via lip rounding decoupling to isolate anterior oral cavity size;
  - Preventing nasal leakage on `-ng` codas.
- **3.5 Register vs. Range**: Maintaining baseline spoken register while dynamically navigating conversational span.

### Chapter 4: Objective Acoustic Metrics, Measurement & Norms
- **4.1 Clinical Evaluation Protocol**: 3-tier evaluation framework (Subjective Well-being TVQ-MtF $\to$ Auditory Perception $\to$ Acoustic Biofeedback); valid recording protocol (unclipped, quiet environment, sustained `/i/` & `/a/`).
- **4.2 In-Depth Acoustic Metrics**:
  - **Resonance Spacing $\Delta F$ & Effective Tract Length $L$**: $\Delta F = \frac{c}{2L}$;
  - **Fundamental Frequency $F_0$ & Floor Baseline**: Target speaking range $170\sim 220\text{ Hz}$, minimum floor $150\text{ Hz}$;
  - **Vocal Weight $H_1-H_2$**: Normal conversational range $+2\sim +6\text{ dB}$;
  - **Twang / Pharyngeal Strain Index**: $2.2\sim 4.2\text{ kHz}$ to $0.4\sim 1.5\text{ kHz}$ energy ratio;
  - **Stability & Perturbation**: Jitter $< 1.0\%$, Shimmer $< 3.8\%$, HNR $> 20\text{ dB}$.
- **4.3 Comprehensive Baseline Norms Table**: Cross-referencing cisgender female, cisgender male, and transfeminine target benchmarks.
- **4.4 Minimal Detectable Change (MDC) & Bone Conduction**: Filtering day-to-day 5 Hz measurement noise; understanding why your recorded voice sounds different from what your skull hears.

### Chapter 5: Compensatory Fatigue, Medical Boundaries & Vocal Safety
- **5.1 Swallowing Constriction & "Silent Half-Yawn"**: Breaking suprahyoid muscle lock-in.
- **5.2 Ventricular Constriction & "Silent Giggle"**: Retracting the false vocal folds.
- **5.3 Falsetto & Over-Breathiness Pitfalls**: Limitations of uncoordinated CT phonation and glottal gap fatigue.
- **5.4 Medical Boundaries & Red Flags**:
  - Vocal hygiene (hydration, reflux prevention, rest);
  - Realistic expectations regarding Wendler Glottoplasty and CTA surgeries;
  - **⚠️ Clinical Red Flags**: Instant cessation triggers (persistent hoarseness $>2$ weeks, sharp throat pain, blood in sputum, aphonia) requiring immediate ENT laryngoscopy.

### Chapter 6: Progressive Practice Prescription & Daily Generalization
- **Phase 0 · Somatic Release**: Postural alignment, Alexander awareness, hyoid suspension freedom.
- **Phase 1 · SOVTE**: Lip trills, straw phonation, and closed-lip humming ($[m]$) to decouple weight without laryngeal tension.
- **Phase 2 · Anterior Resonance Bridge ($m \to i$)**: Tongue-palate anchoring, high-vowel "clothing vowel" focus, oral-pharyngeal shaping.
- **Phase 3 · Mandarin Tonal Glide Words**: Curated practice lists for level tone, Tone 3 half-dip floating, Tone 4 soft landing, and neutral tone feather hovering.
- **Phase 4 · Real-Life Generalization**: Ordering coffee, phone calls, social situation habituation, micro-habit softenings (sighs, giggles, throat-clearing alternatives).

## Core Acoustic Metrics Reference

| Metric | Target Range (Transfeminine) | Clinical Measurement | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Speaking Fundamental Frequency ($F_0$)** | $170\sim 220\text{ Hz}$ (approx. F3 to A3) | Continuous speech mean | Basic pitch foundation; floor $\ge 150\text{ Hz}$ |
| **Vocal Weight Indicator ($H_1 - H_2$)** | $+2.0\sim +6.0\text{ dB}$ | Sustained vowel $/i/$ or $/a/$ | Glottal contact thickness; avoids pressed voice ($<0\text{ dB}$) |
| **Resonance Formant Spacing ($\Delta F$)** | $1120\sim 1260\text{ Hz}$ | Sustained vowel $/i/$ (first 4 formants) | Corresponds to effective vocal tract length $13.5\sim 15.5\text{ cm}$ |
| **Second Formant ($F_2$) on $/i/$** | $\ge 2450\text{ Hz}$ ($2500\sim 2850\text{ Hz}$) | Vowel $/i/$ peak | Anterior oral cavity volume and tongue elevation |
| **Sibilant $/s/$ Spectral Centroid** | $6500\sim 8200\text{ Hz}$ | Unvoiced snake hiss $[s]$ | Articulatory sharpness and front constriction |
| **Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR)** | $> 20\text{ dB}$ | Sustained clean vowel | Overall vocal efficiency, clarity, and non-breathiness |

## Mandarin Phonetic Localization Highlights

- **Tone Floating (软上声高位托底)**:
  In standard Mandarin, Tone 3 (上声) dips down to 1 in traditional 5-point notation (214). When speaking fast, beginners plunge into male-register vocal fry ($<100\text{ Hz}$). The Tone Floating protocol instructs keeping Tone 3 as a floating 211 dip, softly passing above $150\text{ Hz}$ without laryngeal drop.
- **Soft Landing (去声浅降)**:
  Tone 4 (去声 51) has the largest pitch drop. Hard impact triggers Thyroarytenoid contraction. Practicing 53 or 52 soft landings with gentle pre-voicing airflow maintains resonance height.
- **Laminal Articulation (舌叶化)**:
  Mandarin retroflexes ($zh, ch, sh$) often cause speakers to pull the tongue root back, darkening the hypopharynx. Laminal articulation uses the blade of the tongue, preserving pharyngeal space.
